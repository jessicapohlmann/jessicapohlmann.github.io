About

I am a PhD candidate in philosophy at Rutgers University. I work in metaphysics and the philosophy of physics, with a focus on mereology, ontological dependence, metaphysical issues in quantum mechanics, and the emergence of spacetime in quantum gravity.

Before coming to Rutgers in 2024, I was a PhD candidate at the Dianoia Institute of Philosophy at Australian Catholic University, and I completed an MPhil at the University of Adelaide. I am also the editorial assistant for the Australasian Journal of Philosophy.

Works in progress

  1. Metaphysics and Representation: The Case of Quantum Non-separability

    Quantum entanglement is often taken to show that the world is non-separable, and so to support metaphysical conclusions about holism and mereological structure. I argue that such conclusions cannot simply be read off the quantum formalism, because whether a quantum state counts as separable depends on two representational choices the formalism does not settle: how a system is decomposed into subsystems, and the mathematical framework and rule by which the states of those subsystems are combined. I show that metaphysical lessons from quantum non-separability are available only once further metaphysical work has been done.
